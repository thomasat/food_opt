FOOD OPTIMIZER — START HERE

Food Optimizer suggests which formulations to make next and learns from
your results. You set up ingredients and measurements, make a round,
record what you measured, and repeat.

WHAT YOUR COMPUTER NEEDS
- A Mac with an Apple chip (M1 or later — any Mac from late 2020 onward).
  Not sure? Click the Apple menu (top left of your screen), choose
  "About This Mac", and look at the "Chip" line.
- macOS 13 Ventura or newer.
- About 6 GB of free disk space once set up.
- An internet connection the first time you open the app. After that,
  no internet is needed.

HOW TO INSTALL
1. Drag the Food Optimizer icon onto the Applications folder icon
   next to it.
2. Open your Applications folder and double-click Food Optimizer.
3. The first time, the app finishes installing itself. It downloads
   about 1 GB. This usually takes under a minute; on a slow network, a
   few minutes. The window shows a progress bar the whole way.
   Leave it open. When it finishes, Food Optimizer opens, ready to use.
   If your Mac asks whether you are sure you want to open it, click Open.

TRYING IT OUT
- The app comes with a sample project: a plant-based burger with eight
  ingredients and two panel scores. On the first screen, click
  "Try the sample project" to explore before setting up your own.
- Your ingredients and your measurements are two tables on the Set up
  tab, and you type straight into them: change a cell, type a new row
  on the empty line at the bottom, or take a row out. Nothing reaches
  your project while you type. When there is something to save,
  "Save changes" lights up, and "Discard changes" beside it puts the
  table back the way it was.
- To fix an ingredient at one amount, type the same number in Lowest
  and Highest. Widen the range again and the app is free to move it.
- Starting from a spreadsheet? On the Set up tab, open "Or upload an
  ingredients file" to download the template, fill it in, and upload it
  from the same place.

USING IT DAY TO DAY
- "Batch size (g)" on the Make a round tab is what one formulation adds
  up to. Change it and the round on screen, and the sheets you print,
  are worked out again at that size.
- The round sheets open in Excel. They are locked except the shaded
  cells, so you can only write where the app reads: what you measured,
  Not scored, a note, the Lot each ingredient came from, and
  "Actual (g)" when the balance gave you something other than the
  amount printed. Upload the file again and the app takes all of it.
- To open the app again, open Food Optimizer from Applications, like
  any app. It runs in its own window.
- To quit, close the window.
- Your FoodOptimizer folder (File › Open Projects Folder) is one click
  away. Need bigger text? View menu › Bigger Text.

YOUR DATA STAYS ON YOUR COMPUTER
- Your formulations and results never leave your Mac. The app
  does not connect to any website or service, and it collects no
  statistics.
- Your project files are saved in your FoodOptimizer folder, in your home
  folder (in Finder: click the Go menu, then Home). You can see, copy or
  delete them at any time.
- The one-time setup only downloads the software the app runs on. It
  sends nothing of yours anywhere.

NEED HELP?
Write to the Food Intelligence Lab at
https://github.com/thomasat/food_opt/issues. Describe the problem in
words, and do not attach project files, saved copies or formulations, because
that page is public.

Attaching the app's log file helps us find the problem faster. Inside
the app, choose Show Log File from the Help menu and it finds the file
for you. If the app will not open, find it in Finder instead: click the
Go menu, choose "Go to Folder...", paste the line below, press Return,
and attach the file that opens:
  ~/Library/Application Support/FoodOptimizer/launcher.log
