FOOD OPTIMIZER — START HERE

INSTALL
1. Drag Food Optimizer into Applications.
2. Open Food Optimizer from Applications.
3. Keep the window open while the first-time setup downloads the required
   software (about 1 GB). Progress appears in the window.
   Setup time depends on your connection and computer.

You need a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer), macOS 13 Ventura or newer,
about 6 GB of free space, and internet for first-time setup. Check your chip
in Apple menu → About This Mac. You can use the app offline after setup;
an app update may need another software download.

Updating? Quit the old app, then replace it in Applications. Your saved
projects are stored separately and are retained.

TRY THE SAMPLES
“Open the burger example” opens an example set-up on 1 · Set up, with
illustrative values and no results.

For real results:
1. Click “Try the yogurt pilot.” It opens “Yogurt pilot - Round 6 to
   score” on 4 · Review. It is a real yogurt-style pilot: 15 formulations
   scored, and Round 6 made and waiting for its results.
2. Go to 2 · Make a round and open “Or upload results from a file.”
3. Click “Browse files,” open your FoodOptimizer folder and choose
   “Round 6 results (pilot).xlsx”: it is already there. Or click
   “Download the pilot's Round 6 results,” then upload that file.
4. Review the imported values and click “Save uploaded results.”
5. Open 3 · Results for Round 6's ratings against their targets, then
   4 · Review for the best so far.
6. Under What next?, “Start Round 7” has the app suggest Round 7.
   “See the pilot's Rounds 7–10” opens “Yogurt pilot - all 10 Rounds”:
   what the pilot made next.
To try it again, open Manage project and click “Start this example over.”
Your version is kept in Saved copies.

BURGER EXAMPLE
Click “Open the burger example” to open “Example setup - plant-based
burger” on 1 · Set up. Its values are illustrative and it has no
results. It shows calculations (hydration water as 2.2 × the textured
proteins, remaining water as the rest), pre-mixes, a process setting,
records and a formulation total.

YOUR FIRST ROUND
1. Set up your own project (see below). On 2 · Make a round, click
   “Generate formulations” and choose how many to make.
2. Download the round sheets (Excel). The workbook has these tabs:
   • Round overview — the plan and shared preparation method.
   • Preparation — pre-mixes to prepare, only when the round has them.
   • Results — measurements and any actual amounts you want to record.
   Tabs are at the bottom in Excel and at the top in Numbers. If a link
   does not open in Numbers, select the named tab directly. Ticking
   "Include individual formulation pages for printing" gives a different
   file instead: one sheet per formulation, with the round sheet and a page
   for each pre-mix.
3. Prepare each formulation separately and enter your measurements in
   the workbook's input cells. Return to Make a round, upload the workbook,
   review the imported values, and click Save uploaded results. Editing the
   workbook alone does not update the app. You can also type results
   directly into the app.
4. Review the results and start another round. The app uses your saved
   results to suggest what to try next.

SETTING UP YOUR OWN PROJECT
Create a project in the sidebar. Type into the ingredient and measurement
tables, then click Save changes. Use the same Lowest and Highest to keep
an amount or setting fixed. Use different values to let the app vary it.
“Import ingredients” provides a spreadsheet template and an upload option.

“Pre-mix: keep proportions fixed” keeps the same ingredient percentages.
“Blend: vary each ingredient” lets the app choose each ingredient amount
separately, so both the proportions and the combined amount can change
between formulations.

“Preparation and records” holds the batch size, steps shared by all
formulations, references, and recording options. Lot is entered per ingredient in Make a round → Ingredient lot numbers
or in the workbook. Vendor and SKU are entered in the Ingredients table.
“Record changes from the planned amounts or settings” adds workbook cells
for what you actually used in each formulation; leave unchanged values blank.
Choose “Add your own recording field” for other records, such as
Operator or Expiry date. Choose whether to record a value for each
formulation/trial or each ingredient in the round. Enter values in the
app or on the workbook’s Results tab. These records do not affect scores.
Use Measurements and targets for outcomes you want scored. Ingredient limits are optional restrictions
such as a maximum fat content; the sample limits its ingredient total. An empty
minimum or maximum adds no restriction on that side. Missing ingredient
property values must be filled in before generating with a property limit.

For a fermentation study, add numeric process settings such as temperature
and time. A project containing only process settings uses trials and does
not need a batch size. Named categories such as strain are not varied by
this version. Different time points can be separate measurements, such as
pH at 6 h and pH at 24 h.

You can edit an unrecorded formulation in the round table before making it.
Use actual amounts/settings to record what you made if it differs from the
plan. New projects start with optional records turned off.

YOUR FILES AND HELP
Projects are saved on your Mac in ~/FoodOptimizer. File → Open Projects
Folder opens that folder. Use “Save a copy of this project” before a major
change. “Clear project contents,” under Manage project, empties the project
while keeping its name and saving a copy first; it is not needed to start
a new round.

Open the app again from Applications. Close its window to quit.
View → Bigger Text increases the text size.

REVIEWING RESULTS
Results opens on the last Round's results: Overall scores and ratings
against their targets: orange above a target, purple below. How the score is
worked out shows one formulation's points. Use View formulation to
inspect another recorded formulation, including one without a score. Amount and % of
formulation appear together. Percentages exclude process settings and are
unavailable for ingredients in different units. All formulations compares
results in the app; Show amounts adds ingredient amounts to that table.

EDITING CALCULATIONS
Calculation sits beside the ingredient name. Type directly in the cell,
or select Edit calculation for a larger editor with ingredient suggestions.
Choose Vary between limits, Fill to total, or Calculate an amount.
Batch size is the total ingredient amount for one formulation. Fill to total
adds the remaining amount. Use the chosen calculation,
then select Save changes. Examples explain arithmetic, not recommended ratios.
